Why this split

  • Each muscle gets hit once a week with enough volume to grow back what you lost during the break.
  • Cardio days burn extra calories without beating up the muscles you trained the day before.
  • Lifting and cardio alternate, so no day leaves you too sore to show up the next.
  • After 6 to 8 weeks, if you want more, swap Saturday cardio for a second Push or Legs day.

Two real meals, then the cook's dinner

Keep your morning milk coffee. Lunch is a 5-minute plate from your prepped chicken, rice or eggs. A small meal before the gym, a shake after, and the cook handles dinner. Pick options and the totals update.

What to ask the cook

The cook only makes veg, so chicken and eggs are your job at lunch and pre-gym. The one rule for her: every dinner has a veg protein dish, plus 2 rotis and a bowl of curd. Ask for less oil and ghee.

  • Rotate: paneer bhurji or sabzi, soya chunk sabzi, chana or rajma, then thick moong or masoor dal.
  • Paneer and soya give the most protein. Keep a pack of soya chunks in the kitchen for her.
  • On plain dal-sabzi days, add 100 g of your own chicken on the side, or have the protein coffee in the morning.
  • You could say: "Didi, dinner mein roz ek protein item banana, paneer, soya ya chana. Tel thoda kam, aur ek katori dahi."

Your 1 kg chicken

  • That's about 6 lunches of 150 g raw.
  • Raw marinated chicken is good for 1 to 2 days in the fridge. Freeze the rest today in 150 g bags and move one to the fridge the night before.
  • Air fry 2 to 3 portions at a time. Cooked chicken keeps 3 days in the fridge.
  • Rice: cook 1 cup raw at a time (about 3 servings). Cool it and refrigerate within an hour; eat within 2 days.

Weekly shopping

  • 1 kg chicken breast, 24 to 30 eggs (for your own meals)
  • For the cook: 400 g paneer, soya chunks, chana or rajma
  • 1 kg rice, 1 kg curd, toned milk, whey
  • Brown bread, bananas, cucumber, onion, tomato
  • Roasted chana for when you're hungry between meals

The fix for skipping lunch: set a 1:30 alarm and treat lunch like a meeting. If you truly have no time, have the protein coffee and 2 eggs instead. Something with protein always beats nothing.

Quick cooking

Everything here uses only the air fryer, one pot of rice or one pan of eggs. Batch the chicken and rice twice a week and lunch takes 5 minutes.

What to expect

  • Aim to lose 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week. Slower is fine at your size; it keeps the muscle.
  • The scale may barely move in the first month while you regain muscle. The waist tape is the better signal.
  • Belly fat comes off last for most people, and you can't target it with crunches. It goes as overall fat drops.
  • Rough timeline: visible change in 6 to 8 weeks, a clearly flatter stomach in 4 to 6 months.

How to run it

Lifting

  • Double progression. Pick a weight you can do for the bottom of the rep range. When you hit the top of the range on every set, add 2.5 kg (or the next dumbbell).
  • Stop each set with 1 to 2 good reps left. No grinding to failure on squats or bench.
  • Weeks 1 and 2 back: use about 70% of your old weights. Soreness after a long break is normal.
  • Write down weight and reps for every set. Beat last week by one rep or a little weight.

Cardio

  • Zone 2 means you can talk in full sentences but wouldn't want to sing. Roughly 120 to 140 bpm.
  • Incline walking is the best fit: easy on knees, burns well, doesn't wreck your legs for Friday.
  • Hit 8,000 steps a day on top. Walk during calls; it matters more than people think on a desk job.

Check-ins

  • Weigh 3 mornings a week after the toilet, before food. Use the weekly average.
  • Measure your waist at the navel every Sunday morning.
  • If both are flat for 2 weeks, drop 150 kcal (about 100 g less rice) or add 2,000 steps.
  • Sleep 7+ hours. Short sleep makes fat loss much harder.

Missed a day?

  • Missed a lift: do it on the next cardio day and move cardio to Sunday.
  • Missed cardio: walk 45 minutes outside in the evening or skip it. Never skip lifting for cardio.
  • Busy week: the three lifting days are the minimum. Protein every day, no matter what.
